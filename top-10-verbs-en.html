Page 1: Introduction to the Top 10 Core Verbs

Welcome to Part 1 of our 2-part master verb guide for A0–A1 and A2 English learners! When you start learning English, you don't need thousands of words to start speaking. Frequency research demonstrates that just 10 core verbs carry up to 80% of daily spoken output. This 12-page issue gives every core verb its own dedicated page with clear grammar rules, practical sentence frames, real-world examples, and common traps to avoid.

Page 2: Verb 1 — “To Be”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1🪪The verb that introduces you

To Be

Use be to introduce yourself and describe people, places and situations.

With this verb, you can…introduce yourself · describe feelings and states · say where someone or something is
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

🙋

Introduce yourself

I'm Maya. I'm a student.

😊

Describe feelings and states

We're happy. He is tired.

📍

Say where someone or something is

The keys are on the table.

🌦️

Talk about age, jobs, time and weather

I'm 20. She is a doctor. It's cold.

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: am · is · are

I

am / I'm

I'm ready.
He / She / It

is / isn't

She is at home.
You / We / They

are / aren't

They are friendly.
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

She ___ happy today.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Ask for details: Where are you? How old is she? What is your teacher like? Use there is / there are to say what exists: There is a café nearby.

Page 3: Verb 2 — “To Have”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1🎒The verb for what’s yours

To Have

Use have to talk about what you own, people in your life, features, needs and routines.

With this verb, you can…talk about possessions · talk about family · describe features or problems
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

🎒

Talk about possessions

I have a blue bag. She has a bike.

👨‍👩‍👧

Talk about family

We have two children. He has a sister.

🧑

Describe features or problems

She has brown eyes. I have a headache.

🍳

Talk about meals

I have breakfast at seven.

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: have · has

I / You / We / They

have

We have a question.
He / She / It

has

He has a dog.
Negative / question

don't / doesn't have · Do / Does…have?

Does she have a car?
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

He ___ two sisters.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Have got is also common for possession, especially in British English: I've got a bike. Have you got any brothers or sisters? Say Does she have…? or Has she got…?

Page 4: Verb 3 — “To Think”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1💭The verb for ideas and opinions

To Think

Use think to share an opinion, say what you believe, or explain what is on your mind.

With this verb, you can…give an opinion · say what you believe · share an idea
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

💭

Give an opinion

I think this film is good.

🤔

Say what you believe

I think he's at home.

💡

Share an idea

What do you think?

🗣️

Agree or disagree politely

I don't think so. I think you're right.

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: think · thinks

Opinion

I think + idea

I think English is useful.
Question

What do you think of…?

What do you think of this song?
Negative

I don't think…

I don't think it's difficult.
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

I ___ this film is great.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Think about means consider something: I need to think about it. Think of can ask for an opinion: What do you think of the new café?

Page 5: Verb 4 — “To Know”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1🧠The verb for facts and familiarity

To Know

Use know for information, familiarity with people or places, and skills you have learned.

With this verb, you can…talk about facts · talk about people and places · say you have information
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

📚

Talk about facts

I know the answer.

👋

Talk about people and places

Do you know Anna? I know this town.

🧭

Say you have information

I don't know where it is.

🛠️

Talk about skills

She knows how to swim.

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: know · knows

Facts

know + information

I know his name.
People / places

know + person / place

We know London.
Skills

know how to + verb

He knows how to cook.
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

She knows ___ to swim.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Use know if/whether for yes-or-no information and know where/why/when for details: Do you know if the shop is open? Do you know where the station is? Embedded questions use statement word order.

Page 6: Verb 5 — “To Like”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1❤️The verb for favourites

To Like

Use like to talk about preferences, hobbies and the things you enjoy.

With this verb, you can…say what you enjoy · talk about hobbies · talk about food preferences
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

❤️

Say what you enjoy

I like music. She likes cats.

🎨

Talk about hobbies

We like swimming.

🍕

Talk about food preferences

He doesn't like onions.

💬

Ask about preferences

Do you like coffee?

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: like · likes

Like + noun

like + thing

I like this song.
Like + -ing

like + activity

I like reading.
Questions / negatives

Do / don't · Does / doesn't

Does she like tea?
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

He ___ playing football.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Both like doing and like to do are possible. Compare I like coffee (general preference) with I'd like a coffee (polite request now).

Page 7: Verb 6 — “To Want / Would Like”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1🎯The verb for wishes and polite requests

To Want / Would Like

Use want to express a need or desire. Use would like to sound more polite.

With this verb, you can…say what you want · talk about goals · order politely
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

🎯

Say what you want

I want a new phone.

📖

Talk about goals

I want to learn English.

☕

Order politely

I'd like a tea, please.

🙋

Ask what someone wants

What would you like?

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: want · wants · would like

Want + thing

want + noun

I want some water.
Want + action

want to + verb

We want to go home.
Polite request

would like + noun / to + verb

I'd like a menu, please.
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

I'd ___ a glass of water, please.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Would you like…? offers or invites someone: Would you like some tea? / Would you like to come with us? Answer Yes, please or No, thank you.

Page 8: Verb 7 — “To Go / Be Going To”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1🧭The verb for movement and plans

To Go / Be Going To

Use go for movement and regular destinations. Use be going to for plans and intentions.

With this verb, you can…say where you travel · talk about routines · make plans
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

🚶

Say where you travel

I go to work by bus.

📅

Talk about routines

She goes to school every day.

🗺️

Make plans

We're going to visit Paris.

🚌

Say how you travel

They go by train. I go on foot.

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: go · goes · am/is/are going to

Routine

go / goes + destination

He goes to the gym.
Transport

go by + transport

We go by car.
Future plan

am / is / are going to + verb

I'm going to cook tonight.
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

We are ___ to visit Paris.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Use go home without to: I go home at six. Be going to can describe a plan or a prediction based on evidence: Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.

Page 9: Verb 8 — “Can”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1💪The little word for ability and permission

Can

Use can to talk about ability, ask for permission and make simple requests.

With this verb, you can…say what you can do · say what isn't possible · ask for help
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

🏊

Say what you can do

I can swim. She can drive.

🚫

Say what isn't possible

I can't come today.

🙋

Ask for help

Can you help me, please?

🚪

Ask for or give permission

Can I sit here? You can use my pen.

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: can · can't

Positive

subject + can + base verb

He can speak English.
Negative

can't + base verb

We can't stay.
Question

Can + subject + base verb?

Can I open the window?
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

She ___ speak three languages.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Could you…? is often more polite than Can you…? Can does not change with he/she/it and takes the base verb without to: She can swim.

Page 10: Verb 9 — “To Eat”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1🍎The verb at the heart of food and meals

To Eat

Use eat to talk about food, meals, eating habits and choices.

With this verb, you can…talk about food · describe meal routines · explain food choices
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

🍎

Talk about food

I eat fruit every day.

🍳

Describe meal routines

He eats breakfast at seven.

🥗

Explain food choices

I don't eat meat.

🍽️

Talk about eating out

We eat out on Fridays.

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: eat · eats

Routine

eat / eats + food or meal

She eats lunch at work.
Question

Do / Does + subject + eat…?

Do you eat fish?
Plans

want to / would like to eat

I'd like to eat outside.
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

He ___ lunch at one o'clock.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Useful combinations: eat breakfast, eat lunch, eat dinner, eat healthy food and eat out (eat at a restaurant). Ask: What do you usually eat for breakfast?

Page 11: Verb 10 — “To Drink”

COSYMAGAZINE · ENGLISH A0–A1💧The verb for drinks and everyday habits

To Drink

Use drink to talk about beverages, daily habits, and offering or ordering something to drink.

With this verb, you can…talk about daily habits · say what you drink · offer a drink
01

Where this verb comes alive

Everyday situations where this little verb does a big job.

💧

Talk about daily habits

I drink water every day.

☕

Say what you drink

He drinks coffee in the morning.

🥤

Offer a drink

Would you like something to drink?

🧾

Order in a café

Can I have a glass of water, please?

02

Say it naturally

Notice these useful patterns and expressions.

Key forms: drink · drinks

Routine

drink / drinks + beverage

She drinks tea.
Question

Do / Does + subject + drink…?

What do you drink at breakfast?
Polite order

Can I have…? / I'd like…

I'd like an orange juice, please.
03

A quick language check

Can you spot the expression that sounds right?

I ___ water every day.

A2 Go further: extra language and grammar

Drink is the action; a drink is a beverage: I drink tea every day. Can I have a cold drink? In a café, Would you like…? offers something; I'd like… makes a polite request.
