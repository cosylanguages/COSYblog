Page 2: Verb 1 — "To Be" (am / is / are)

The verb to be is the most foundational verb in English. At A0–A1 level, you use it to talk about who you are, how you feel, where you are, your age, job, nationality, weather, time, and daily states.

2. Main Usages & Practical Sentence Frames

  • A. Identity & Names (Subject + be + noun): • Examples: Hello, my name is Damian, he is my brother Alex, Lady Gaga is his favourite singer, and they are my best friends from school. This is my new book.
  • B. Jobs & Roles (Subject + be + a/an + job): • Examples: I am an English teacher, I am not a student, and my brother is a hospital doctor, so he is not an IT specialist. Lady Gaga is a famous singer and an actress. • ⚠️ ATTENTION: Always use a or an before singular countable jobs (a doctor, an actress, an IT specialist).
  • C. Age (Subject + be + age): • Examples: I am 29 years old today, my younger brother is 20 years old, and my parents are 55. How old are you? • ⚠️ ATTENTION: In English, we say "I am 20 years old" or "I am 20". NEVER say "I have 20 years".
  • D. Nationality & Origin (Subject + be + nationality / from + country): • Examples: I am French, I am not Italian, and my brother is Spanish because our family is from Paris. They are Italian tourists.
  • E. Descriptions & Characteristics (Subject + be + adjective): • Examples: My friend Dylan is tall and he is very funny. The classic movie is black and white, and my bedroom is clean today. The birthday cake is delicious.
  • F. Feelings & Physical States (Subject + be + state): • Examples: I am very happy to meet you, but we are extremely hungry and thirsty after work, and Dylan is very tired.
  • G. Location & Spatial Prepositions (Subject + be + location): • Examples: Today I am at home, my brother is in the kitchen, my keys are on the wooden table, and my colleagues are at work. • Key Prepositions: in (in the room), on (on the table), under (under the bed), next to (next to the café), behind (behind the house), in front of (in front of the school), between (between two shops), near (near the station), at (at work/school).
  • H. Time, Weather, and Dates (It + is + time/weather/date): • Examples: It is Friday morning, and it is 11 o'clock. The weather is sunny but it is cold outside. Today is October 23.
  • I. Demonstratives & Possession (This/That is, These/Those are): • Examples: This is my leather bag, that is her younger sister, these are our study books, and those people are my parents. • ⚠️ IMPORTANT: To be identifies who something belongs to ("This bag is mine" / "This is my car"). It does not replace have ("I have a car").
  • J. Material, Measurements & Numbers: • Examples: This dining table is made of solid wood, the total price is €10, the subway station is 2 kilometres away, and my room is 4 metres wide.

5. Wh- Question Words & Patterns

  • What: What is your full name? / What is this object on the desk?
  • Who: Who is she? / Who are those people over there?
  • Where: Where are you right now? / Where is my smartphone?
  • When: When is your birthday party?
  • Why: Why are you so sad today? / Why is he angry?
  • How: How are you feeling today? / How are your parents?
  • How old: How old is your younger brother? — He is 18 years old.
  • How much: How much is this blue leather bag? — It is €20.
  • What... like?: What is your new teacher like? — He is friendly and funny. (Asks about character/description). • Note: Contrast with How is your grandfather? — He is fine today. (Asks about health/well-being).

6. Essential Structures & Daily Expressions with "To Be"

  • A. There is / There are (Existence): • Singular: There is a white cat in the garden. / There isn't a free chair here. / Is there a bank near here? • Plural: There are two chairs in the kitchen. / There aren't any students in class. / Are there any open shops?
  • B. Essential Fixed Expressions with "To Be": • be right / be wrong: You are right about the answer, but I am wrong. • be afraid / be sure: She is afraid of dogs, but I am sure everything is fine. • be interested in / be good at: He is interested in modern music, and I am good at English. • be late / be early / be ready / be busy / be free: We are late for work, dinner is ready, and I am free tomorrow.

7. Practice: Check Your Understanding

❓ Choose the correct form: 'She ___ 25 years old and she ___ a doctor.'

In English, we use 'to be' for age ('She is 25') and for professions ('she is a doctor'). Never use 'has' for age.

❓ Is she at home? — Choose the correct positive short answer:

In positive short answers, never use contractions. Always use the full form: 'Yes, she is.'

Page 3: Verb 2 — "To Have" (have / has)

To Have is essential for possession, relationships, bodily symptoms, daily routines, and obligations (have to).

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Present Simple Forms: I/You/We/They have | He/She/It has.
  • Possession & Relationships: I have a new car in the garage. / She has two brothers. / We have a big house in Paris.
  • Bodily States & Illnesses: I have a bad headache today. / He has a cold. / She has a high fever.
  • Daily Routines & Meals: I have breakfast at 8 AM, have lunch with colleagues, have dinner, have a coffee, have a warm shower, have a short break.
  • Obligation (have to + verb): I have to work today. / She has to go home early.
  • British vs American (Have got): I have got a friendly dog (= I have a dog). / Have you got a fast car?

Page 4: Verb 3 — "To Think"

To Think allows you to express your opinion, share your thoughts, and ask others for their view.

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Present Simple Forms: I/You/We/They think | He/She/It thinks.
  • Expressing Opinions (I think that...): I think it is a good idea. / I think learning English is fun. / She thinks this movie is great.
  • Short Responses: I think so. (I agree / I believe so) | I don't think so. (I disagree).
  • Asking Opinions: What do you think about this idea? / What do you think of this film?
  • Key Collocations: think twice before deciding (be careful), think about the problem (consider), think of a solution (have an opinion/idea).

Page 5: Verb 4 — "To Know"

To Know is used for facts, familiarity with people/places, and skills (know how to).

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Present Simple Forms: I/You/We/They know | He/She/It knows.
  • Knowing Facts: I know the right answer. / I don't know the address. / She knows where the keys are.
  • Familiarity (People & Places): Do you know Paul from work? / I know this historic city very well.
  • Skills & Abilities (know how to + verb): I know how to drive a car. / She knows how to cook delicious Italian pasta.
  • Useful Daily Expressions: Let me know when you arrive (inform me), As far as I know, the shop is open (to my knowledge), You know... (filler phrase).

Page 6: Verb 5 — "To Like"

To Like expresses preferences, tastes, enjoyment, and polite offers (would like).

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Present Simple Forms: I/You/We/They like | He/She/It likes.
  • Like + Noun: I like hot coffee in the morning. / She likes outdoor sports. / Do you like classical music?
  • Like + Verb-ing: I like reading books in bed. / He likes swimming in the sea.
  • Polite Requests & Offers (Would like): I would like a cup of green tea, please. / Would you like to join us for dinner?
  • Appearance & Resemblance (look like): He looks like his father. / What does your new house look like?

Page 7: Verb 6 — "To Want / Would Like"

To Want and Would Like express desires, intentions, and polite requests.

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Direct Desires (Want + Noun / Verb): I want a glass of cold water. / I want to learn English fluently. / Where do you want to go tonight?
  • Polite Standard (Would like + Noun / Verb): I would like to order lunch now, please. / She would like a fresh salad.
  • Negative Forms: I don't want to be late for class. / He doesn't want to go home yet.
  • Common Phrases: if you want to come, I want to know the truth, we would like to see the museum.

Page 8: Verb 7 — "To Go / Be Going To"

To Go expresses movement, travel, routines, and future intentions (be going to).

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Present Simple Forms: I/You/We/They go | He/She/It goes.
  • Movement & Prepositions: I go to work by train. / She goes to school every morning. / We go to the park on Sundays.
  • Exceptions without 'to': go home, go abroad on holiday, go downtown, go grocery shopping.
  • Future Intentions (be going to + verb): I am going to eat dinner with friends. / They are going to buy a new car next month.
  • Transport Phrases: go on foot (walk), go by bus, go by train, go by car.

Page 9: Verb 8 — "To Can" (Modal Verb)

Can is a modal verb expressing ability, permission, and polite requests.

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Modal Rule: Can never changes form (no '-s' for he/she/it). It is followed directly by a base verb without 'to'.
  • Ability: I can speak three languages. / She can swim very fast in the pool. / He can play the acoustic guitar.
  • Negative Ability (cannot / can't): I can't drive a manual car. / She can't come to the party tonight.
  • Requests & Permission: Can you help me with this bag? / Can I have a glass of water? / You can sit here near the window.
  • Useful Phrases: as fast as I can, I can do it, Can I ask a quick question?

Page 10: Verb 9 — "To Eat"

To Eat is used for food, daily meals, dining habits, and food preferences.

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Present Simple Forms: I/You/We/They eat | He/She/It eats.
  • Daily Food: I eat fresh fruit every morning for breakfast. / He eats lunch at 1 o'clock at work.
  • Questions & Desires: What do you want to eat for dinner? / Do you eat vegetarian food?
  • Phrasal Verbs & Collocations: eat out at an Italian restaurant, eat healthy food, let's find something to eat.

Page 11: Verb 10 — "To Drink"

To Drink covers beverages, hydration, offering drinks, and social café situations.

Forms & Sentence Frames

  • Present Simple Forms: I/You/We/They drink | He/She/It drinks.
  • Daily Beverages: I drink two litres of water every day. / He drinks black coffee in the morning. / We drink hot tea in winter.
  • Offers & Polite Phrases: Would you like something to drink? / What would you like to drink with your meal?
  • Noun Usage: a hot drink on a cold day, a cold drink in summer, let's have a drink together.
