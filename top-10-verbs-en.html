Top 10 Essential Verbs in English for Absolute Beginners (A0–A1 / A2)

Welcome to Part 1! Here are 10 very useful English verbs. With them you can say many things. Part 2 has 10 more verbs (read Part 2). You can see all the verb forms on COSYtools.

How to learn

  1. Read one verb at a time.
  2. Say the examples out loud.
  3. Change one word. Say I like tea, then I like music.
  4. Do the quiz at the end.

1. be (am / is / are)

  • Meaning: to say who or what you are, how you are, or where you are.
  • Forms: I am · you are · he / she / it is · we / you / they are
  • Examples: I am a student. · She is 25. · They are at home. · We are happy.
  • Careful! Say I am 25, not I have 25 years. Short forms: I'm, you're, he's, she's, we're, they're.
  • Useful phrases: be on time · be quiet · You are right.

2. have (have / has)

  • Meaning: to own something, to have family, or to have a health problem.
  • Forms: I have · you have · he / she / it has · we / they have
  • Examples: I have a car. · She has a sister. · I have a headache.
  • Careful! Say she has, not she have. For no, say I don't have a car.
  • Useful phrases: have breakfast · have a shower · Have a good day!

3. think

  • Meaning: to say your opinion, or to have an idea in your head.
  • Forms: I think · you think · he / she / it thinks
  • Examples: I think so. · I think it is good. · What do you think?
  • Careful! I don't think so means No, I am not sure. Use think about + thing: I think about my family.
  • Useful phrases: think about · think of · I think so

4. know

  • Meaning: to have information, to know a person, or to know how to do something.
  • Forms: I know · you know · he / she / it knows
  • Examples: I know the answer. · Do you know him? · I know how to go there.
  • Careful! Say I know, not I am knowing. The short answer is I don't know.
  • Useful phrases: Let me know. · I don't know. · Do you know?

5. like

  • Meaning: to think something is good, or to enjoy it.
  • Forms: I like · you like · he / she / it likes
  • Examples: I like coffee. · She likes eating fruit. · Do you like going home?
  • Careful! Say I like music, not I am like music. After like you can use a noun (coffee) or a verb with -ing (eating).
  • Useful phrases: I like it very much. · Do you like it?

6. want / would like

  • Meaning: to say what you need. Would like is more polite.
  • Forms: I want · you want · he / she / it wants · I would like (I'd like)
  • Examples: I want water. · I would like tea, please. · Where do you want to go?
  • Careful! Say I want to go, not I want go. In a shop or café, I would like is nicer than I want.
  • Useful phrases: I would like to go home. · Can I have water, please?

7. go / be going to

  • Meaning: to move to a place, or to say your plan.
  • Forms: I go · you go · he / she / it goes · we / they go. Plan: I am going to + verb.
  • Examples: I go to school. · She goes home. · We are going to eat.
  • Careful! Say go home (no to), but go to school. For a plan: I am going to eat, not I go to eat.
  • Useful phrases: go home · go shopping · go on foot · be going to eat

8. can

  • Meaning: to say you are able to do something, or to ask if it is OK.
  • Forms: I can · you can · he / she / it can (no -s!) · negative: I cannot / I can't
  • Examples: I can go now. · You can eat here. · Can I have water?
  • Careful! Say he can go, not he cans. Say I can go, not I can to go.
  • Useful phrases: Can I have ...? · I can't go. · Can you go?

9. eat

  • Meaning: to put food in your mouth and have a meal.
  • Forms: I eat · you eat · he / she / it eats
  • Examples: I eat fruit. · What do you want to eat? · Let's eat out.
  • Careful! Say she eats, not she eat. Use eat for food and drink for liquids.
  • Useful phrases: eat out · something to eat · eat breakfast

10. drink

  • Meaning: to take water, tea, coffee or other liquids.
  • Forms: I drink · you drink · he / she / it drinks
  • Examples: I drink water. · He drinks coffee. · Would you like something to drink?
  • Careful! Drink is also a noun: a hot drink. Say drink tea, not eat tea.
  • Useful phrases: drink water · a hot drink · something to drink

Put it together: a short dialogue

  • Ana: Hi Ben! Are you hungry?
  • Ben: Yes, I am. I want to eat.
  • Ana: I know a good café. Do you like pizza?
  • Ben: Yes, I like pizza very much. I think it is good.
  • Ana: Great! We are going to go there. Can you go now?
  • Ben: Yes, I can. I would like a coffee. Can we drink it first?
  • Ana: Yes, we can. I have a card.

Grammar help for beginners

  • Simple sentence: subject + verb + something. I eat fruit.
  • Yes / no questions: Are you hungry? Can you go? Do you like tea?
  • He / she / it: most verbs add -s (likes, wants, eats). Have becomes has and go becomes goes. Can never changes.

Quiz: choose the correct answer

❓ She ___ a sister.

With he / she / it, have becomes has.

❓ I ___ 25 years old.

We use be for age: I am 25.

❓ He ___ coffee.

With he / she / it, add -s: he likes.

❓ I want ___ home.

After want, use to + verb: I want to go home.

❓ Can she ___ here?

After can, use the base verb with no -s: Can she eat here?

❓ Do you ___ the answer?

After Do you, use the base verb: Do you know?

❓ We are ___ to eat now.

For a plan, use be going to: We are going to eat.

Now you speak

  1. Say three true sentences about you with have, like and want.
  2. Ask a friend: Do you like ...? Can you ...? Do you want to ...?
  3. Tomorrow: cover this page. Say one sentence for each of the 10 verbs.
