Saturday Mistakes: 5 Common English Mistakes & How to Fix Them

A practical breakdown of 5 frequent English speaking mistakes, natural conversational upgrades, and podcast guidance (Segment: 00:00 – 22:18).

Welcome to our new series Saturday Mistakes! Making mistakes is an essential part of becoming a fluent, confident speaker. In this edition, we break down 5 common English mistakes that intermediate speakers make, alongside instant conversational upgrades.

Mistake 1: "I am agree with you"

In English, agree is a verb, not an adjective. You do not need the auxiliary verb am.

❌ Common Mistake: I am agree with your opinion.
✅ Correct: I agree with your opinion.
🚀 Natural Upgrades: I couldn't agree more! • I'm totally with you on that.

Mistake 2: "I have 25 years old"

Many speakers translate literally from languages like French, Spanish, or Italian (avoir/tener/avere). In English, we use the verb to be for age.

❌ Common Mistake: I have 25 years old.
✅ Correct: I am 25 years old. or I'm 25.
🚀 Natural Upgrades: I'm in my mid-twenties.

Mistake 3: "I am waiting you since 2 hours"

Two big errors here: wait requires the preposition for, and durations of time use for rather than since.

❌ Common Mistake: I am waiting you since two hours.
✅ Correct: I've been waiting for you for two hours.
🚀 Natural Upgrades: I've been waiting for ages!

Mistake 4: "Explain me this rule"

The verb explain cannot take a direct personal object without to. You explain something to someone.

❌ Common Mistake: Can you explain me this grammar rule?
✅ Correct: Can you explain this grammar rule to me?
🚀 Natural Upgrades: Could you walk me through this rule?

Mistake 5: "I am boring in this class"

Confusing -ed and -ing adjectives changes your entire meaning! -ed describes how you feel, while -ing describes the cause.

❌ Common Mistake: I am boring during the meeting. (This means you make others bored!)
✅ Correct: I am bored during the meeting.
🚀 Natural Upgrades: I'm finding it hard to stay engaged.
