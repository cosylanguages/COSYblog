Welcome to Part 2! Here are 10 more useful verbs. Some are pairs or groups, because English has different words for similar ideas. Read Part 1 first if you need it. You can see all the verb forms on COSYtools.

How to learn

  1. Read one group at a time.
  2. Look at the small difference between the verbs in the group.
  3. Say the examples out loud, then make your own.
  4. Do the quiz at the end.

1. do / make

  • Meaning: do is for tasks and activities. make is for creating something.
  • Forms: I do · he / she / it does · I make · he / she / it makes
  • Examples: I do my homework. · She makes tea. · We make a cake.
  • Careful! Say do homework, not make homework. Say make a mistake, not do a mistake. Do also helps in questions: Do you like tea?
  • Useful phrases: do homework · make breakfast · make a mistake

2. speak / talk / say / tell

  • Meaning: speak is for a language. talk is to have a conversation. say is for the words. tell is for a person.
  • Forms: speak / talk / say / tell · he / she / it speaks / talks / says / tells
  • Examples: I speak English. · Let's talk. · Say hello. · Tell me.
  • Careful! You say something, but you tell someone: Tell me, not Say me. Say talk to a person.
  • Useful phrases: speak English · talk to a friend · say yes · tell a story

3. look / watch / see

  • Meaning: look (at) is to turn your eyes on purpose. watch is to look at something that moves. see is what your eyes get.
  • Forms: look / watch / see · he / she / it looks / watches / sees
  • Examples: Look at this. · Watch a film. · I see a bird.
  • Careful! Say look at, not look this. Watch is for films, matches and things that move.
  • Useful phrases: look for · watch out! · See you soon!

4. listen / hear

  • Meaning: listen (to) is to give your ear on purpose. hear is when sound comes to you.
  • Forms: listen / hear · he / she / it listens / hears
  • Examples: Listen to music. · I hear a bird. · Can you hear me?
  • Careful! Say listen to music, not listen music. You listen to the teacher, but you hear a noise.
  • Useful phrases: listen carefully · I am glad to hear that.

5. must / have to

  • Meaning: Both mean you need to do something. must feels strong, like a rule. have to is very common in speech.
  • Forms: I must · he / she / it must (no -s!) · I / we / they have to · he / she / it has to
  • Examples: I must go. · We have to go now. · She has to eat.
  • Careful! Don't have to means not necessary: You don't have to eat. Must not means not allowed: You must not drink here.
  • Useful phrases: have to go · I must go · you don't have to

6. take / give / get

  • Meaning: take is to hold something and move it with you. give is to put something in another person's hand. get is to receive something, or to become.
  • Forms: take / give / get · he / she / it takes / gives / gets
  • Examples: Take a coat. · Give me the book. · I get home at six.
  • Careful! Give is for another person: Give me water. Get can mean become: I get tired.
  • Useful phrases: take care · get tired · get ready

7. live

  • Meaning: to have your home in a place.
  • Forms: I live · you live · he / she / it lives
  • Examples: I live in London. · She lives with her family. · We live together.
  • Careful! Say live in + city, and live with + people. Say she lives, not she live.
  • Useful phrases: live in · live with · live together

8. wait

  • Meaning: to stay until something comes or happens.
  • Forms: I wait · you wait · he / she / it waits
  • Examples: Wait for the bus. · Can you wait a minute? · Wait here.
  • Careful! Say wait for the bus, not wait the bus.
  • Useful phrases: wait for · wait in line · I can't wait!

9. understand

  • Meaning: to know what something means.
  • Forms: I understand · you understand · he / she / it understands
  • Examples: I understand. · Do you understand me? · I don't understand.
  • Careful! Say I understand, not I am understanding. When you don't know, say: I don't understand.
  • Useful phrases: I don't understand. · Do you understand?

10. ask / answer

  • Meaning: ask is to say a question. answer is to give a reply.
  • Forms: ask / answer · he / she / it asks / answers
  • Examples: Ask a question. · Answer the phone. · Ask for water.
  • Careful! Say answer the question, not answer to the question. Say ask for + thing: ask for help.
  • Useful phrases: ask for help · answer the phone · ask a question

Put it together: a short dialogue

  • Mia: Hello, Sam. Can you hear me?
  • Sam: Yes, I can. Where are you?
  • Mia: I am at home. I live near the park. I want to ask a question.
  • Sam: OK. Ask me.
  • Mia: I don't understand the homework. Can you tell me?
  • Sam: Yes. Look at page five. You must do the exercise today.
  • Mia: Wait, I want to take my book. Now I see it. Thank you!
  • Sam: Listen. Give me your answer tomorrow, and we can talk.

Grammar help for beginners

  • Order of words: subject + verb + something. I ask a question.
  • Short answers: Yes, I can. No, I don't.
  • Must and can never change: he must, she can. Have to changes: she has to.

Quiz: choose the correct answer

❓ I ___ my homework every day.

We use do with homework: do homework.

❓ She ___ breakfast for us.

We use make when we create something, like breakfast.

❓ Please ___ me the answer.

Tell needs a person after it: tell me.

❓ ___ at the board, please.

Use look at for turning your eyes to something on purpose.

❓ Can you ___ me? The music is loud.

Hear is about sound reaching you. Listen needs to: listen to me.

❓ We ___ go now.

With we, use have to. Must is not followed by to.

❓ Wait ___ the bus here.

The pattern is wait for + thing.

❓ She ___ in Paris.

With he / she / it, add -s: she lives.

  2. Ask a friend: Can you hear me? Do you understand? Where do you live?
