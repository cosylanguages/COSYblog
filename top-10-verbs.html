🗺️ The 10 Essential Core Verbs (CEFR A0–A1 Focus)

These 10 essential action anchors power everyday speech across all 14 official languages. Mastery of these verbs unlocks simple survival communication, basic questions, personal identity, and daily needs.

1. To Be — Existence, location, identity, states.
2. To Have — Possession, bodily states, obligations.
3. To Do / Make — Actions, activities, creating things.
4. To Go — Movement, direction, travel plans.
5. To Come — Origins, arrivals, invitations.
6. To Say / Tell — Speech, sharing information, asking questions.
7. To See / Look — Visual perception, understanding.
8. To Know — Facts, skills, familiarity with people/places.
9. To Want — Needs, wishes, ordering food & drinks.
10. To Like / Give — Preferences, appreciation, exchange.

🧬 Language Family Nuances & Cross-Family Insights

🌳 Indo-European Family

Sub-groups: Romance (FR, IT, ES, PT), Germanic (EN, DE), Slavic (RU), Hellenic (EL), Celtic (BR), Armenian (HY).

Romance languages rely heavily on auxiliary verbs (be/have) for compound tenses and feature explicit subject-verb endings. Germanic languages split modal and action verbs clearly, while Slavic and Armenian utilize aspectual pairs (imperfective vs. perfective).

🏹 Turkic Family

Languages: Tatar (TT), Bashkir (BA), Chuvash (CV).

Turkic languages are agglutinative: verb stems stay intact while tense, person, and negation are attached via ordered suffixes at the end of the word. Possession uses existential predicates (bar / pur = "there is") rather than a standalone "to have" verb.

🏔️ Kartvelian Family

Language: Georgian (KA).

Georgian features polypersonal agreement (the verb marks both the subject and the object simultaneously). Possessive and emotional verbs use indirect inversion: "I have a car" is expressed literally as "To me exists a car".
